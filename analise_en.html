The data point Oracle's Elixir doesn't capture — scrim win rate

FURIA holds a ~60% win rate against RED in scrims, meaning they actually have an edge in practice. The collapse to 1/7 in official games is therefore even more revealing: FURIA does not lose due to lack of technical ability, but due to an inability to replicate the same level under competitive pressure. This definitively rules out roster quality as the root cause and points to a systemic problem in managing the early-to-mid transition, specifically around Baron control.

Original hypothesis vs reality

The initial hypothesis, that RED adapts picks/bans faster between games, identifies a real symptom but not the root cause. The data points to a problem that predates the draft: FURIA lacks a consolidated system for converting early game advantages into Baron Nashor control. RED won 6 Barons across the 7 games. Those 6 RED Barons correspond exactly to FURIA's 6 losses. FURIA's single Baron corresponds to their single win.

01
Methodology
Player rating system · Oracle's Elixir 2026

Each player receives a score from 0 to 10 per game, calculated from their metrics compared against all 10 players in the same game (not just those in the same role). Metrics and weights are calibrated per position. The final score is the median across all games played, resistant to outliers and capturing the typical player, not their best or worst moment.

STEP 01 · RAW VALUE
Metric calculation
Absolute metrics (kills, gold, damage) are normalized per game minute to eliminate bias from long or short games. Ex.: DPM = damagetochampions / gamelength_min
STEP 02 · 0–10 NORMALIZATION
Game-wide pool
Each metric value is normalized against all 10 players in the game. The highest value receives 10, the lowest receives 0. Formula: (v − min) / (max − min) × 10. Scores are not comparable across roles.
STEP 03 · WEIGHTING + MEDIAN
Final score
Game score = sum of normalized metrics × position weight. Final player score = median across all games. Standard deviation complements the median by measuring consistency.

Note on cross-role comparability

Scores are not comparable across positions. A support with a 6.2 and a jungler with a 6.2 are not equivalent, as the metrics and scales differ by design. Supports compete on vision and participation metrics with higher relative variance; junglers on kill participation and gold diff in a more contested pool. Valid comparisons are always within the same position. Standard deviation complements the median by measuring consistency: high deviation means "feast or famine"; low deviation means reliability.

The vision paradox

FURIA consistently destroys more wards than RED, with a 1.2–1.6× advantage per game. In the only WIN (LB PO G3), the margin is 1.65×. The problem is not lack of vision, it is decision-making with the available vision. The informational advantage does not translate into movement restrictions or Baron prerequisites.

07
Qualitative Evidence
The 4 games of the MD5 elimination series, what the data does not capture
G1
LOSS
33min · Single critical point
Non-conventional vision, the Scuttle Crab
Tutsz moved through an area covered by the Scuttle Crab's vision, a passive effect of the neutral objective after 20min, not a conventional ward. He was picked off; the team died in the resulting teamfight. JoJo on Rakan had controlled vision well up to that point, securing the 4th dragon with proper setup. The loss was caused by disregarding a non-conventional vision source, independent of the support matchup.
G2
LOSS
20–27min (CP1) · 29min (CP2)
3 pickoffs from disregarding vision + unnecessary teamfight
CP1: FURIA lost 3 pickoffs (2× Tutsz and 1× Ayu on Corki, his third loss in 3 games with the champion in 2026) by moving through areas not covered by the vision JoJo had established on Nami. The team failed to account for the fact that Nami creates coverage differently than a tank.

CP2: With insufficient vision, FURIA engaged in a Soul Dragon teamfight that did not need to happen. Teamfight loss → Baron to RED → game over.
G3
WIN
23–24min · Correct execution of prerequisites
JoJo intercepts frosty · Baron with numerical advantage
JoJo (Alistar) found and intercepted frosty (Bard) actively contesting the vision space, generating a controlled pickoff. With numerical advantage, FURIA started Baron, even if not perfectly, relying on Tatu hitting the smite in a 50/50 contest. Baron secured; team eliminated; game over. This win was the result of an individual read at a specific moment, not a system FURIA executes consistently.
G4
LOSS
24min (CP1) · 27min (CP2)
Teamfight without setup + teleport neutralized by opponent vision
CP1 (24min): Insufficient vision on both sides, FURIA secured the dragon but lost the teamfight.

CP2 (27min): Premature initiation before Tutsz's teleport arrived to defend the top T2. The teleport was executed through an inefficient path because RED had superior vision control in the area, and the flanking maneuver was completely neutralized.

JoJo — #1 support in the league vs. matchup losses

JoJo is the best support in CBLOL Cup 2026 by the metric. His median of 8.04 drops to 6.10 in the 7 matchups against frosty (−1.94), the largest contextual drop of any player on the roster. This is not an individual limitation: it is a specific matchup where the type of pick determines who physically controls the vision space before Baron. On Alistar (G3), JoJo wins the contest. On Rakan, Nami and Milio, he loses the space before any teamfight even begins.
